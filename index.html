IN MEMORY

Remembering those we lost.

This page is dedicated to the people of Nasir and Upper Nile State who lost their lives during the 2025 conflict. Names are only published when reliably documented in authoritative sources. We welcome corrections and additions through the Submit page.

Privacy and dignity notice

We do not publish personal details that could endanger survivors, witnesses, or family members. This memorial does not claim to be complete; many deaths went unrecorded. If you have corrections or additional information, please use the Submit page. All submissions are reviewed before publication.

Documented casualties: Upper Nile, early March 2025

Human Rights Watch documented that at least 58 people were killed and 17 others severely burned following government aerial bombardments in Nasir, Longechuk, and Ulang counties of Upper Nile State in early March 2025. This figure covers three counties and is attributed to HRW. Individual names have not been independently published in verified reports available to this site.

Radio Tamazuj reported that at least 21 civilians, including two women and two children, were killed in an aerial bombardment in Nasir town on the night of 16 to 17 March 2025. This figure is attributed to local officials and residents as reported by Radio Tamazuj.

MSF's Ulang hospital treated 34 patients for airstrike injuries from Nasir and Kuich from 3 to 21 March 2025. This figure represents patients seen at that facility; total casualties are unknown.

Source: Human Rights Watch World Report 2026

UNMISS colleague killed during evacuation

UNMISS reported that a member of a helicopter crew was killed during an evacuation effort during the clashes in Nasir. UNMISS described this loss as "particularly poignant" for peacekeepers returning to the area for the first time since the incident.

Source: UNMISS April 22, 2026

Community voices

The following accounts are drawn directly from published UN and news reporting. They are attributed to named sources.

“Some people were scared to return when conflict arose in this area. I was one of them. I recently arrived back, feeling afraid, but as an elderly person, I decided to come anyway. I believe those who stayed behind will eventually follow me.”

Samuel Gai, Nasir Community Leader, quoted by UNMISS, April 22, 2026

“I ran away with my child. I could see the village burning from afar. They set houses on fire. They burned my grandmother inside the tukul. The elderly stayed behind; they could not run with us. They killed the elders.”

A woman displaced and treated at an MSF facility in Chuil, Jonglei state, quoted in MSF report “They Killed Them While We Were Running,” May 19, 2026

“Crimes against humanity are unfolding in plain sight.”

Edmund Yakani, civil society activist, quoted by Radio Tamazuj, March 17, 2025

COMMUNITY SUBMISSIONS

Share what you know.

Eyewitness accounts, photographs, corrections, missing names, and community memories all help build a more complete record. Your safety matters to us.

⚠ Privacy and safety notice

Do not submit information that could endanger you, your family, or witnesses. Do not include home addresses or contact details of individuals who may be at risk. All submissions are unverified until reviewed by our editorial team. We will not publish personal details that could cause harm.

Guidelines

  • Be as specific as possible about dates, locations, and what you saw.
  • Include your source of information if it is not personal knowledge.
  • Corrections are welcomed and will be reviewed carefully.
  • Do not submit material that could endanger people still at risk.
  • All submissions are unverified until reviewed.
  • We may contact you for clarification; contact details are never published.

WHO WE ARE

News with a purpose.

Nasir Youth Community is a community first news and historical documentation project focused on Nasir, Upper Nile State, South Sudan.

01

Our mission

To make community stories visible, document reported events with source transparency, and support informed public conversation through responsible journalism.

02

Our values

Accuracy, fairness, respect, transparency, and a commitment to listening to people whose experiences are often overlooked. Victim dignity always comes first.

03

Our approach

We summarise external reports, link to original sources, attribute casualty figures to the reporting organisation, and clearly distinguish incident dates from publication dates.

04

Sources we draw on

Human Rights Watch, Radio Tamazuj, UNMISS, MSF (Médecins Sans Frontières), The Guardian, and other reputable international and South Sudanese outlets. We do not fabricate reports.

05

Editorial notes

Every Nasir Crisis article carries an editorial note explaining what the source covers, what it does not, and where figures may be contested or incomplete.

06

Corrections

We welcome corrections. If you believe something is inaccurate, use the Submit page or email us directly. We correct promptly and transparently.

Placeholder content notice

Some community and editorial stories on this site are placeholder content for layout demonstration. They are clearly labelled. The Nasir Crisis timeline entries are based on verified external reporting and are source linked.
